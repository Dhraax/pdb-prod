## 2026-08-28 — Wrapped saving throws share one implementation

**What changed.** `inc_spells` remains the public facade and now delegates save
work to the specialised `inc_spellsave`. That include owns the native Fortitude,
Reflex and Will dispatch, DC bounds, success and immunity feedback, death-save
feedback and the spell-specific Spellcraft compensation. The modern
`gsSPSavingThrow` route calls that core directly.

The public `MySavingThrow` signature is unchanged for its 171 legacy callers,
but its body is now only a compatibility adapter into the same core. Shadow
Defence's school rule moved beside the other school rules in
`inc_spellschool`; both entry points call that one implementation. No spell or
ability keeps a second copy of the wrapped saving-throw algorithm.

Focused compilation covered all 142 files identified as direct consumers. The
140 executable scripts succeeded with no errors; `cls_ing_lib` and
`x0_i0_spells` were skipped because they are includes without an executable
`main`.

## 2026-08-31 — Five artificer defects, and none of them a balance decision

**Improve Artifact III did nothing, and Born in Lantan added damage it should
not.** Every specialization but Artillerist read feats 1771, 1772 and 1773 for
its three extra dice. 1771 is Born in Lantan; the three Improve Artifact feats
are 1772, 1773 and 1774. So an alchemist or an armorer with Born in Lantan was
rolling a die he had not earned, and one with Improve Artifact III was missing
the die he had. The Artillerist branch already read the right three, and the
comment above both branches already said which feats it meant.

**The Elixir of Knowledge finally does what it is for.** The line that set its
flag was commented out, so three things never happened: the reuse lock never
engaged, the delayed cleanup deleted a variable that was never there, and the
`+4` caster level in `pb_nivellanzador` never applied. Only the `+4` Intelligence
was landing. The flag is set as a boolean, which is what every reader compares
against and what the existing delayed delete expects.

**Saving against the Gas Bomb no longer pays better than failing.** A creature
that made its Fortitude save took the full acid damage and a creature that failed
took half, on entry and on every tick. They are now the right way round: full on a
failure, half on a save. The poison is unchanged and still only lands on a
failure.

**The Gas Bomb's poison tier stops asking about a paladin.** Two of its three
level tests were `GetLevelByClass(60, oCaster)`, and class 60 is
`Paladin_Antiguos`, not the Artificer. An artificer with sixteen or more levels of
that class fell through every tier and got no poison at all. The tests now read
Artificer levels only: up to 12, 13 to 16, and 17 or more.

**Sticky Goo's lock lasts as long as its penalties.** The slow and the attack
penalty run for `caster level / 2` **rounds**, and the variable that stops the
goo being reapplied was deleted after `caster level / 2` **turns** - ten times
longer. A target was immune to a second application for most of a fight after the
first one had worn off. Both now use rounds.

**Pending in-game test.**

The two feats first, because they change numbers: an alchemist or an armorer with
Born in Lantan and no Improve Artifact should now deal one die *less* than before,
and one with Improve Artifact III one die *more*. Check an artillerist too and
confirm nothing about him moved.

The Elixir of Knowledge: drink it and confirm you cannot drink it again until it
expires, and that spells cast while it is up behave as four caster levels higher -
duration and damage are the easiest to read. Confirm the lock lifts on its own,
and that it also lifts on relog.

The Gas Bomb: stand in it and save, then fail. Failing must hurt more and must be
the only way to catch the poison. Then an artificer of 12, of 15 and of 18, to see
the three poison tiers; before this, one with levels in Paladín de los Antiguos
could get none.

Sticky Goo: hit a target, wait for the slow to wear off, and hit it again. The
second application must work.

**Still owed.** Eighteen further items from the same review need a decision
before anything is written, because in each case the implementation and the
published description say different things and only the owner can say which one
is right.

reset, the logout, death, and the server restart, because those live in variables
written into the character file.

A reset warlock therefore kept the essence bonus to their difficulty, worth up to
nine with Utterdark, without needing any invocation feat to apply it. Reported in
play.

The reset now clears everything the class writes on its own character: the
essence and its damage type, the Empower or Maximize setting, a pending Horrid
Blow, the pending Hellrime Blast, the Walk Unseen record, the shadow teleport
state, and the seven variables the undead shape leaves behind. It strips the
Horrid Blow property from **every equipped slot** rather than the right hand
only, because the invocation falls back to gauntlets when there is no melee
weapon and the character may have swapped weapons since arming it.

**Horrid Blow no longer turns away a monk holding a bow.** Arming it read the
right hand and refused outright when what it found was a ranged weapon, without
ever looking at the gloves the character would actually be punching with. The
ranged case now falls through to the arms slot, the same fallback an empty hand
already took. Nothing in either slot still refuses, with the same message.

The shadow teleport state is the one worth naming. `RUTASOMBRAS` is read by the
general teleport library, which heals half of maximum hit points whenever it is
set - from any teleport, not only the warlock's own. Left behind by a reset it
was a free half-heal on the character's next trip anywhere.

**It also gives back the invocation slots.** The invocation window keeps its own
bookkeeping on the character - which invocation occupies which slot of each
grade, and how many of each grade are spent. The reset removed the feats and left
every one of those standing, so the window went on believing the slots were
filled by invocations the character no longer had. All thirteen are cleared now.

Witch Sight keeps its slot, because it is the one invocation the reset does not
take back. Freeing the slot of an invocation the character still holds would let
him pick two more dark invocations on top of it.

Three warlock variables are deliberately left alone because they are not written
on the warlock: the repel marker on whoever is being pushed, the grapple marker
on a held target, and the ownership variables on a summoned undead.

**A lesser essence no longer weakens a greater shape.** Choosing an essence
replaced the invocation's own difficulty bonus instead of raising it, so a
warlock with Brimstone active had their cone two points easier to resist and
their doom four points easier than with no essence at all. The essence is now a
floor: it raises the difficulty where it is higher and leaves it alone where it
is not.

**Chilling Burst's damage respects spell resistance.** Only the knockdown was
behind the resistance check; the damage landed on a target that had resisted the
invocation entirely. The saving throw still decides only the knockdown, which is
the essence rider rather than the blast.

**Devour Magic's temporary hit points respect the same cap its dispel does.**
The cap of twenty was applied after the effect was built, so a warlock past
twenty gained his real level in temporary hit points while the dispel check still
ran at twenty. This is the twin of the Voracious Dispelling defect below, in the
same shape, in the sibling script; it was found while closing that one and left
standing until now.

**Voracious Dispelling's damage respects its own cap.** The dispel check is
capped at caster level 15 and the damage was built before that cap, so a warlock
above sixteen dealt more damage while checking the same fifteen.

**Retributive Invisibility's concealment ends with the invisibility.** They were
two separate effects with the same duration, so attacking removed the
invisibility and left the fifty percent concealment running for the rest of it.
They are now one effect.

**Eldritch Chain jumps at least once, and no more at low level than at mid.**
Below level five it granted two extra targets; between five and nine it granted
one. The floor is now one, so the chain always jumps and never gives a novice
more reach than a veteran.

**Eldritch Cone stops being able to hit its own caster.** A precedence mistake
meant the caustic essence walked past the self-exclusion. Another filter was
masking it, so nobody had seen it.

**An essence damage-over-time can no longer make a creature permanently immune.**
The guard that stops a second rider stacking was cleared by the last tick of the
chain, and that tick belongs to the caster - so a warlock who logged out or died
mid-chain left the guard set for ever and that creature could never be affected
by that essence again. A cleanup now runs on the target itself.

That cleanup is scheduled for the full length the chain could run, and a chain
that ends early - the target saves, or dies - releases the guard sooner. The
timeout would otherwise arrive later and delete the guard of whatever chain had
started in between, which is the stacking it was meant to prevent. Each chain now
stamps the guard with its own number and the timeout only releases a guard that
still carries it.

  blindly.** A count that went negative meant the next entry landed on zero, so
  the wall dealt its first hit and then never ticked at all.
- **`wrap_on_cl_leave` cleared the warlock's count on logout and not the
  wizard's.** A count that survives a logout is a delayed chain with nothing left
  to stop it — the exit script that would have cleared it never ran. This is the
  wall that kept burning.
- **Undead were meant to take double on entry and did not.** The line read
  `nDamage * 2;`, which computes a value and throws it away. They doubled inside
  the chain, which assigned it, and never on the first hit.

There is one counter now, in the library, used by all four scripts. Both walls
are cleared on logout.

**Also:** a wall whose caster is gone stops rather than burning on with no caster
level behind its damage. And `iCarisma` is gone from the warlock wall — it was
computed on every entry and never read. In 3.5 a warlock's Charisma sets the save
DC of an invocation, 10 + half warlock level + Cha, and contributes nothing to
damage; Wall of Perilous Flame allows no save, so there was no DC for it to set.

**What did not change.** The damage, the timing, who is a valid target, when
spell resistance is rolled, and the per-round chain itself. An earlier attempt
today replaced the chain with an area heartbeat and was reverted in full because
it kept changing behaviour that had not been agreed.

**Test.** Five things, and only the first is quick.

1. **Undead, first hit.** Walk an undead into a wall. The first hit should double,
   as later ones already did.
2. **Extra exits then re-entry.** Stand in a wall, leave, then cross its edge a
   few more times so more exits than entries fire. Go back in: it must still tick
   every round. Before the floor, the count could go negative and the next entry
   left it at zero, so the wall hit once and never again.
3. **Logout inside each wall.** Log out standing in a wizard's wall, come back,
   and confirm you are not being burned by a wall that is no longer there. This
   was already right for the warlock's and wrong for the wizard's.
4. **The caster leaves.** Have the caster log out or die while somebody stands in
   the wall. The burning should stop rather than continue at a reduced,
   ownerless damage.
5. **Two walls, and a target the wall cannot touch.** With an ally, or a target
   that resists the spell, walk it into one wall and then into a second. It must
   take the second wall's damage normally — being unaffected by the first must
   not count as standing in it. This is the branch an earlier version of this
   change got wrong.

**Still owed.** Nothing to repack. `nw_s0_wallfirec.nss` is kept: no reference
anywhere, 306 bytes, never executed. It now opens with a header saying so, and
naming the two traps inside it - an unreachable branch and a flag nothing reads.

## 2026-08-27 — Tenacious Magic gave its bonus to schools it excludes

**What changed.** Greater Dispelling no longer lets Tenacious Magic protect
evocation and transmutation spells.

The feat's own text says the benefit does not extend to spells you cast from
those two schools, and the dispel code checks exactly that - in one of its two
paths. `applyDispelBest` declared the school variable and never filled it, so the
check compared an empty string and always passed. Every school got the bonus
there. `applyDispelAll` was correct and is unchanged.

**Which path you get depends on how you aim, not on which dispel you cast.**
Dispel Magic, Greater Dispelling, Lesser Dispel and Mordenkainen's Disjunction
each call the dispel twice: once for a single target, which takes the correct
`applyDispelAll`, and once per creature in the area, which takes the broken
`applyDispelBest`. So all four spells change, and all four change only when cast
at an area.

The DC itself was already right and stays as it was: 11 plus the caster level,
plus 4 when the feat applies, which is the 15 plus caster level the feat
describes.

**Also deleted, as dead code.** `add_spell_dispel.nss` was a second and worse
implementation of the same rule: it added a flat +5, it read the school from
whatever spell the creature had last cast rather than from the effect being
dispelled, and it wrote its return value onto a different object than the one the
caller read it from, so it could return a stale number left by another script.
None of that ever ran. Its only caller was inside
`GZGetHighestSpellcastingClassLevel`, which nothing in the module calls.

**And the school is now read in one place.** `cerr_newdispel.nss` had its own
`getSpellShool` reading `spells.2da` directly and comparing raw letters. It now
uses `gsSPGetSpellSchool` and the `SPELL_SCHOOL_*` constants, so there is one
implementation of "what school is this spell" rather than two that could drift.
The area of effect path still uses the school string recorded at cast time and is
untouched.

**Test.**

Aim at the ground, not at the character. Targeting a creature directly exercises
the path that was already right and proves nothing.

- A Shadow Adept with Tenacious Magic casts an evocation. Someone without the
  Shadow Weave feat drops an **area** Dispel Magic on them. It must be no harder
  to dispel than any other evocation; before this it was 4 harder. Repeat with
  Greater Dispelling and with Mordenkainen's Disjunction - same expectation, and
  with Lesser Dispel if it can reach.
- The same with a transmutation: 4 easier than before.
- The same with an enchantment, illusion or necromancy spell: still 4 harder,
  unchanged. This is the one that proves the exclusion is reading the school
  rather than switching itself off.
- Now the single-target version of all of the above: unchanged in every case,
  because that path was never wrong.

**Still owed.** The `+2` block beside it is suspect and was deliberately not
touched. Its comment says the caster holds Tenacious Magic while the code checks
the feat on the dispeller, and its school exclusions are the mirror of the block
above it. Working out what it was meant to do is its own change.

**Commits.** `1dc991700`

## 2026-08-27 — Spells stopped inheriting the last spell's school

**What changed.** A spell's school is now read from `spells.2da`, not remembered
on the caster.

The Shadow Weave feats need to know which school you are casting. The module
kept that in a local variable on the caster that 378 spell scripts wrote and
nothing ever cleared, so a spell whose script never wrote it silently used the
previous spell's school. 215 impact scripts of spells that do have a school
never wrote it, `nw_s0_darkness`, `nw_s0_evards`, `nw_s0_inccloud`,
`nw_s0_sphchaos` and `nw_s0_truesee` among them. Cast a necromancy and then
darkness and darkness collected necromancy's Shadow Weave bonus; cast an
evocation first and your illusion lost its own.

`inc_spellschool.nss` reads the school from the spell itself, so it is right
whatever the script does. The old variable is still honoured for the 3433 rows
of `spells.2da` that carry no school at all - warlock invocations, subradial
spells, feats - where the script remains the only source.

**Also, Pernicious Magic worked on the wrong condition.** The feat gives +4 to
beat the spell resistance of a Weave user, which is every creature that does not
itself hold Shadow Weave Magic. The old script never looked at the target, so it
gave +4 against everything, and it filtered by school, which the feat does not
do. Both halves are corrected. Shadow Weave's own +1 stays mutually exclusive
with it, exactly as before.

`add_spell_penetr.nss` is deleted; its one caller in `nw_i0_spells.nss` now calls
the include directly, which is also how the target became visible to the check.

**How far this reaches, which is less than it sounds.** The school is now read
correctly for every spell, but only one thing reads it yet: the spell penetration
check in `MyResistSpell`. A spell that rolls its resistance through
`gsSPResistSpell` - darkness among them - goes to the engine's plain
`ResistSpell` and is untouched by this. The other three readers of the school,
the DC, the dispel and Shadow Defence, are still the old scripts.

That penetration check also used to be skipped entirely for any caster whose
module caster level matched the engine's, which is most of them, because the
block it lives in exists to correct multiclass caster levels and returns early
when there is nothing to correct. The Shadow Weave modifier is now computed
before that early return and the shortcut is taken only when the modifier is
zero, so the feats work for ordinary casters too. That was already true of the
Spell Penetration feats in the same block and is why this had to change.

**Test.**

- Pernicious Magic against a creature with spell resistance and no Shadow Weave
  feat: +4 on the penetration roll, in any school, on a single-class caster as
  well as a multiclass one.
- The same against a creature that does hold Shadow Weave Magic: no +4.
- Shadow Weave Magic casting an evocation at a creature with spell resistance:
  -1 on the roll.
- A caster with no Shadow Weave feat at all: the roll must be exactly what it was
  before, including for single-class casters, who now enter a branch they used to
  skip.

**Still owed.** Tenacious Magic, Shadow Defence and the DC side are not touched
yet. Tenacious Magic cannot be repaired where it sits: it needs the school of
the effect being dispelled, and its call site knows only the target creature's
caster level. It moves to `cerr_newdispel.nss`, which already walks effects.

**Commits.** `395a55f3a`
